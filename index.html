What I work on

Research focus

⚡

Fast Calorimeter Simulation

Replacing Geant4 with deep generative models for hadronic shower simulation in ECal and HCal detectors, as point clouds with energy and time.

🌊

Diffusion & Flow Matching

Developing and benchmarking diffusion models and flow matching architectures for high-fidelity, geometry-independent shower generation.

🔁

One-Step Generators

Pushing the frontier of inference speed with latent mean-flow generators for pion showers — physics quality at near-zero latency.

📐

Geometry Independence

Training models that generalise across calorimeter geometries and particle types, reducing the cost of re-simulation for new detector designs.

Point-cloud generative models for fast calorimeter simulation across particles and geometries
T. Buss, H. Day-Hall, F. Gaede, G. Kasieczka, K. Krüger, A. Korol, T. Madlener, P. McKeown, M. Mozzanica, L. Valente

A review of point-cloud fast simulation for highly granular calorimeters: CaloClouds3 for photon showers, about 120 times faster than Geant4 on one CPU; CaloHadronic for pion showers across the electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters; AllShowers, which covers twelve particle types in a single, smaller model; and cross-geometry transfer learning, which needs 100 to 1000 times fewer training showers.

Jevons' Paradox and Fast Generative Simulation for HEP: Why Realistic Benchmarking is Essential
T. Buss, H. Day-Hall, F. Gaede, G. Kasieczka, K. Krüger, A. Korol, T. Madlener, P. McKeown, M. Mozzanica, L. Valente

Calorimeter simulation drives much of the computing and energy cost of HEP analyses. Fast generative models make it about 100 times cheaper, but cheaper simulation invites more of it: Jevons' paradox. The paper argues that realistic benchmarks are needed for these speed-ups to turn into real efficiency.

SPADE: Split-and-Delay Embeddings for Autoregressive High-Granularity Calorimeter Simulation
J. Birk, F. Gaede, A. Hallin, G. Kasieczka, M. Mozzanica, H. Rose

An autoregressive transformer that embeds each feature of a multi-feature token separately and delays the feature streams against each other, so that ordinary self-attention learns the correlations inside a token. On ILD photon showers it matches the state-of-the-art AllShowers model and clearly beats its predecessor OmniJet-αC.

CaloClouds3: Ultra-fast geometry-independent highly-granular calorimeter simulation
T. Buss, H. Day-Hall, F. Gaede, G. Kasieczka, K. Krüger, A. Korol, T. Madlener, P. McKeown, M. Mozzanica, L. Valente

Photon showers for the whole barrel of a highly granular detector. Angular conditioning reproduces photons at every incident angle, and position-agnostic training data let one model serve the entire barrel, so it runs inside the full simulation and reconstruction chain. Optimised preprocessing gives a speed-up of two orders of magnitude over Geant4.

CaloHadronic: a diffusion model for the generation of hadronic showers
T. Buss, F. Gaede, G. Kasieczka, A. Korol, K. Krüger, P. McKeown, M. Mozzanica

Extends point-cloud diffusion from electromagnetic to hadronic showers with transformer attention, which captures their richer substructure. It is the first machine-learning model that generates showers holistically across both the electromagnetic and the hadronic calorimeter of a highly granular imaging system, the ILD detector.

JINST 20 · 2025
OmniJet-αC: learning point cloud calorimeter simulations using generative transformers
J. Birk, F. Gaede, A. Hallin, G. Kasieczka, M. Mozzanica, H. Rose

The first generative transformer for calorimeter showers as point clouds. The OmniJet-α tokenizer turns detector hits into sequences of integers; sequences of variable length let showers develop naturally without fixing the number of hits, and the model learns the shower geometry without being tied to a voxel grid.

GNN for Deep Full Event Interpretation and Hierarchical Reconstruction of Heavy-Hadron Decays in Proton–Proton Collisions
J. García Pardinas, M. Calvi, J. Eschle, A. Mauri, S. Meloni, M. Mozzanica, N. Serra

The first prototype of a Deep Full Event Interpretation for LHCb: a graph neural network that identifies, isolates and hierarchically reconstructs all heavy-hadron decay chains in an event at once. Keeping only the relevant particles saves trigger storage and bandwidth under the higher pile-up of LHCb Upgrade I.
